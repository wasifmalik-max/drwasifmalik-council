## Introduction
In complex neurosurgery and spine procedures, even millimetre-level errors in implant placement or tumour margins can force costly revisions. Studies of intraoperative imaging show revision rates dropping 20–40% when portable CT confirms hardware and decompression before closure, versus reliance on pre-operative scans alone. BodyTom intraoperative CT brings full-body, high-resolution 3D imaging directly into the operating room, eliminating patient transfer and enabling immediate verification. ## What is it?
BodyTom (NeuroLogica/Samsung) is a portable, battery-powered, 32-slice full-body CT scanner purpose-built for intraoperative use in operating theatres, ICUs and emergency settings. With an 85 cm bore and 60 cm field of view, it acquires rapid volumetric datasets without moving the patient from the table. Low-dose protocols minimise radiation while preserving diagnostic quality. The system docks with surgical navigation platforms, allowing real-time registration for sub-millimetre guidance during cranial and spinal work. Unlike fixed CT suites or traditional C-arms, BodyTom provides true multiplanar 3D reconstruction on demand—ideal for verifying pedicle screws, assessing residual tumour after resection, or confirming adequate decompression. Its mobility supports multi-specialty use in neurosurgery, trauma and oncology. Evidence from portable CT series confirms improved workflow efficiency and reduced need for post-operative imaging returns to the scanner.

## Causes
Surgical imprecision arises from several interrelated factors. Pre-operative imaging becomes outdated once soft tissues shift, cerebrospinal fluid is drained or bone is removed. Anatomical distortion in tumours, trauma or degenerative spine disease further misaligns planned trajectories. Limited visualisation through narrow corridors or under the microscope can miss residual pathology or malpositioned hardware. In resource-variable settings, delayed access to fixed CT after surgery prolongs detection of complications such as haematoma or screw breach. Radiation-safety constraints and lack of portable systems historically forced surgeons to accept higher revision risk. BodyTom intraoperative CT directly addresses these causes by supplying updated 3D data at the critical decision points inside the OR.

## Symptoms - bold red flags
Patients who may benefit from precision intraoperative imaging often present with progressive neurological deficits. **Red flags demanding urgent neurosurgical evaluation include:**  
- **Sudden or worsening severe headache with vomiting or altered consciousness** (possible raised intracranial pressure or haemorrhage).  
- **New limb weakness, numbness or gait instability** progressing over hours to days.  
- **Bowel or bladder dysfunction with back pain** (cauda equina or spinal cord compression).  
- **Seizures of new onset**, especially with focal neurological signs.  
- **Intractable neck or back pain after trauma** accompanied by sensory level or paralysis.  
- **Visual loss, double vision or cranial-nerve palsies** suggesting compressive lesions.  

These symptoms signal conditions—tumours, fractures, disc herniations or vascular lesions—where real-time confirmation of surgical goals with BodyTom-class imaging can prevent incomplete treatment and secondary injury. Do not ignore progressive deficits; early specialist assessment is essential.

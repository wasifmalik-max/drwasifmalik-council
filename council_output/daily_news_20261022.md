# Laser interstitial thermal therapy (LITT) for tumors

For selected deep-seated, eloquent, or recurrent brain tumors, open craniotomy can carry prohibitive neurologic risk. Laser interstitial thermal therapy (LITT) offers a stereotactic, MRI-guided thermal ablation option that can achieve cytoreduction through a small twist-drill trajectory when conventional resection is judged too hazardous or when patients need a less invasive path after prior surgery and radiation.

## What changed / Why it matters
LITT delivers laser energy via a fiberoptic probe placed under stereotactic guidance; real-time MRI thermometry maps the ablation zone so the surgeon can titrate heat while protecting adjacent critical structures. Contemporary practice focuses on recurrent high-grade gliomas, selected metastases, radiation necrosis that is symptomatic or progressive, and carefully chosen primary lesions in deep or eloquent locations where maximal safe resection is not feasible. The supporting evidence base is largely prospective and retrospective institutional series and multi-center registries rather than large randomized comparisons against open resection or best medical therapy alone. Those series consistently report technical feasibility, acceptable perioperative morbidity in experienced centers, and meaningful local control or steroid-sparing benefit in appropriately selected patients—particularly when the target volume is limited and the goal is local disease control or symptom relief rather than oncologic cure in isolation. Limitations remain clear: edema peaks in the days after ablation, hemorrhage and new neurologic deficits can occur, and durable control still depends on histology, molecular profile, and systemic therapy. LITT is therefore best framed as a complementary tool within a multidisciplinary plan—not a universal substitute for microsurgery when safe gross-total resection is achievable. For the consultant facing a deep recurrent lesion or a patient who cannot tolerate another open procedure, the practical advance is having a reproducible, image-monitored ablation pathway that can be staged, combined with adjuvant therapy, and repeated in selected cases.

## Patient takeaway
If you have a deep, recurrent, or previously irradiated brain tumor and open surgery is considered high risk, ask whether LITT is available at a center with MRI-guided laser experience, how your tumor size and location fit published selection patterns, and what recovery, swelling management, and follow-up imaging will look like. Decisions should rest on imaging review, pathology, and shared goals—not on marketing claims.

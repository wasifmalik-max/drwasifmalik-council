# Leptomeningeal Disease: Neurosurgical Roles in Multimodal Care

Leptomeningeal disease (LMD) is cancer seeding of the pia-arachnoid and CSF pathways. Most durable disease control comes from systemic therapy matched to the primary tumor, involved-field or craniospinal radiotherapy when indicated, and carefully selected intrathecal treatment—not from open resection. Neurosurgery still matters, but mainly as targeted support: securing diagnosis, relieving life-limiting hydrocephalus, and enabling reliable drug delivery when the multidisciplinary plan requires it.

## What changed / Why it matters
Modern LMD pathways treat the neurosurgeon as a selective partner rather than the default first responder. **Biopsy** is appropriate when imaging suggests leptomeningeal involvement yet repeated CSF cytology and circulating-tumor assays remain nondiagnostic, when a treatable mimic (infection, inflammatory meningitis, or a second pathology) must be excluded, or when molecular profiling of tissue would change systemic or targeted options. Sampling is planned to the highest-yield, lowest-morbidity site; indiscriminate meningeal exploration does not improve survival.

**CSF diversion** (typically ventriculoperitoneal shunting, occasionally temporary external drainage) is considered for symptomatic hydrocephalus or refractory intracranial hypertension from impaired CSF resorption—not for “cytoreduction.” Goals are symptom relief, steroid sparing, and making the patient well enough for systemic or radiation therapy. Shunt decisions weigh infection risk, peritoneal seeding concerns in selected histologies, need for programmable valves, and realistic prognosis; diversion is supportive care within a cancer plan, not a stand-alone cure.

**Ommaya reservoir placement** supports repeated, reliable intraventricular access when the oncology team has already chosen intrathecal or intraventricular chemotherapy or selected agents that require consistent CSF dosing. Surgery is justified only if reservoirs will be used; placement without a defined drug protocol adds risk without benefit. Reservoirs complement—never replace—systemic therapy for bulky or parenchymal disease, and CSF flow studies may be needed when obstruction could strand drug in isolated compartments.

Across solid tumors and hematologic malignancies, outcomes track biology and drug/radiation sensitivity far more than operative volume. Surgery helps when it unlocks diagnosis, restores CSF dynamics enough to continue disease-directed therapy, or delivers that therapy safely; it should not delay those modalities in patients without hydrocephalus, diagnostic uncertainty, or an intrathecal indication.

## Patient takeaway
If you or a family member faces possible LMD, ask whether the next step is optimized systemic or radiation treatment, and whether biopsy, a shunt for pressure symptoms, or an Ommaya for planned intrathecal therapy would concretely enable that plan. Neurosurgery is often brief and goal-directed; the long game remains cancer-directed care.

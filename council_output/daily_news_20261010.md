# Cushing disease: remission after TSA

Early, structured biochemical checks after transsphenoidal adenomectomy (TSA) remain the practical hinge between true remission and missed residual corticotroph disease. For ACTH-secreting microadenomas, first-line microscopic or endoscopic TSA aims for selective tumor removal while preserving normal gland. Remission is not declared by imaging alone: postoperative morning serum cortisol, need for glucocorticoid cover, and later urinary free cortisol (UFC) or late-night salivary cortisol define biochemical status and guide who needs salvage before overt recurrence or adrenal crisis risk climbs.

## What changed / Why it matters
Contemporary endocrine–neurosurgical pathways emphasize early postoperative cortisol trajectories (commonly morning values in the first week) together with clinical steroid dependence as markers of remission, then confirmatory UFC or salivary testing off replacement when safe. Persistently non-suppressed or rising cortisol after an adequate resection attempt signals residual disease rather than “slow recovery.” In that setting, timely re-exploration (when MRI or surgical anatomy suggests a targetable remnant), stereotactic radiosurgery for unresectable or invasive residua, and bridging medical cortisol-lowering therapy are the evidence-aligned salvage ladder—before bilateral adrenalectomy is considered for refractory hypercortisolism. Tight coordination reduces two concrete harms after TSA: (1) false reassurance from a “gross-total” operative note while biochemical hypercortisolism continues, and (2) unplanned adrenal insufficiency or crisis when replacement is stopped without a documented low-cortisol nadir and a clear taper plan. Standardized remission definitions also sharpen referral timing for radiosurgery and avoid fragmented follow-up between pituitary centers and local teams.

## Patient takeaway
If you or a family member had TSA for Cushing disease, ask for the written postoperative cortisol results, the steroid plan, and the schedule for UFC or salivary testing. Persistent high cortisol is a reason to reassess residual tumor and salvage options early—not to wait for symptoms alone. Keep stress-dose steroid instructions available until your team confirms durable remission.

# Intraoperative neuromonitoring in tumor surgery

Aggressive cytoreduction for intrinsic brain tumors near the motor strip or corticospinal tract (CST) improves oncologic control only when neurologic function is preserved. Intraoperative neuromonitoring (IONM)—combining motor evoked potentials (MEP), somatosensory evoked potentials (SSEP), and direct cortical/subcortical stimulation mapping—supplies real-time stop rules so resection can be maximized without irreversible injury to descending motor pathways.

## What changed / Why it matters

Contemporary glioma and metastasis practice treats IONM thresholds as actionable resection endpoints rather than optional adjuncts. Transcranial or direct-cortical MEPs continuously sample CST integrity. A sustained amplitude drop of roughly 50% or more from baseline, progressive latency prolongation, or abrupt loss is widely used as a warning or stop signal; irreversible complete MEP loss correlates with lasting motor deficit. SSEPs complement this by tracking dorsal-column–medial lemniscus pathways and can flag vascular or compressive compromise that MEPs alone might miss.

Direct electrical stimulation mapping refines proximity estimates. Cortical mapping identifies primary motor cortex and safe entry corridors. Subcortical stimulation along the resection cavity estimates distance to CST fibers: lower stimulation thresholds indicate closer proximity. Many teams treat progressive threshold decline (commonly into the low single-digit milliampere range) as a cue to halt further tissue removal in that vector, switch to safer corridors, or accept residual tumor rather than risk hemiparesis. These criteria are integrated with neuronavigation and, where available, diffusion-tractography updates, but electrophysiology remains the live functional ground truth when anatomy is distorted by edema or prior treatment.

For the operating team, the practical change is disciplined stop-point discipline: amplitude/latency MEP rules plus falling subcortical thresholds should override pure radiographic “completeness” when CST risk rises. That protects the pathways that determine independent walking, hand use, and return to work—outcomes that matter as much as residual volume on early MRI.

## Patient takeaway

If your tumor sits near movement pathways, ask whether surgery will use continuous MEP/SSEP monitoring and direct stimulation mapping, and how the team defines a stop point when signals change. Clear thresholds help balance tumor removal against the risk of new weakness.
